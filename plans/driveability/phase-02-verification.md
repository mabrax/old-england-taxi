## Runtime and vehicle decisions

The existing scene/session owns one lazy Rapier world and one dynamic vehicle, with no second loop or asynchronous lifetime. Six static colliders are preserved byte-for-byte in their original geometry; one chassis collider and body are added. Static collision inspection excludes the dynamic body so it cannot leave a stale vehicle outline. A failed or ineligible vehicle leaves artifact inspection available. No browser compiler/OSM/report/QA dependency or schema change is introduced.

The installed **@dimforge/rapier3d-compat 0.20.0** declarations govern the API, checked against the [official vehicle reference](https://rapier.rs/javascript3d/classes/DynamicRayCastVehicleController.html) and [rigid-body guide](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_bodies/). The controller updates chassis velocity before `world.step()`. Its unusual forward-axis setter is `controller.setIndexForwardAxis = 2`; up is index 1. The world still advances at 1/60 s, at most five steps per frame, and begins paused.

## Spawn and reset

`vehicle-spawn.ts` deterministically ranks graph segments by geometric length, then distance from local origin, then stable ID, and considers midpoint/quarter/three-quarter hints, stopping after **256 candidates**. IDs never select city-specific rules or coordinates. Graph length/width claims cannot authorize a spawn; actual endpoints provide hints only. Candidate exhaustion, including no usable hints, means driving unavailable even if an undiscovered start might exist.

Each upright candidate validates a conservative rotated whole-vehicle box, including every tyre at maximum steering and full suspension travel. The box extends 0.15 m horizontally and above the chassis. Its swept launch corridor adds **6 m forward and 3 m reverse**, and the entire resulting AABB must retain **10 m additional boundary margin**. The corridor provides low-speed initial maneuvering and stopping room; it does not promise continued pavement at full speed after leaving it. The independent maximum-speed stopping bound is below 5 m, inside the initial 10 m boundary margin.

The existing solid-building/roof occupancy check is applied to that complete AABB, preserving courtyards and rejecting wall crossings, enclosed starts and headroom conflicts. Conservative AABBs can reject a tight rotated fit. Actual road coverage subtracts the indexed triangle union from the full oriented launch rectangle; corners, wheel centres, graph widths or road point samples cannot mask a gap or interior hole. Overlapping triangles cannot cover an unrelated hole by contributing extra area. Geometry uses Float32 positions, Float64 clipping arithmetic, a **1e−7 m² fragment-area tolerance** and at most **256 uncovered fragments**; fragmentation beyond that budget rejects the candidate. This is conservative eligibility, not exhaustive search.

Reset rechecks the saved starting pose against the same pavement and clearance indices. It removes/recreates only the dynamic body and vehicle controller, clearing internal suspension/spin/contact caches, forces, torques, velocity and commands. It snaps previous/current render state, clears the session clock, and pauses. The artifact/world is not reloaded or generated. A failed recheck removes the vehicle and explains unavailability.

The input contract is `{ throttle: [-1,1], steering: [-1,1], brake: [0,1] }`; finite values are clamped and nonfinite commands clear input. It persists until changed. Pause, focus/visibility loss, reset and recovery clear input and disarm it: a **new all-zero command is required before further nonzero input**. A caller repeatedly submitting held throttle across reset receives rejection. A fresh development-exercise button explicitly sends neutral, then the chosen command, for 60 steps before pausing. This is a test harness, not the Phase 03 keyboard/touch design.

Ordinary off-road movement uses the same flat ground and is not snapped to a road. Manual reset handles being stuck or overturned. Before and after each step, nonfinite pose/velocity, a non-unit quaternion, a complete suspension envelope below −2 m/above 20 m, or beyond the horizontal boundary by more than **0.5 m** causes recovery and a paused, neutral-required session. The half-metre threshold is a recovery guard, not permitted chassis penetration: it accommodates the conservative full-travel AABB and prevents normal contact from being mistaken for escape. All four boundary contact tests separately enforce the 3 cm collision tolerance and report actual full-envelope overhang.

## Rendering and inspection

The renderer interpolates consecutive chassis positions and shortest-arc quaternions, wheel steering, unwrapped spin and suspension length. The controller's rays precede integration; separate post-step rays derive visible suspension length at the new body pose without applying a second impulse. Ground-alignment tests check the actual transformed tyre bottom while pitching/turning. At pause/reset the render pose snaps to the current physics pose; stalled frames do not introduce unbounded catch-up.

The vehicle is present by default after eligibility. **Inspect vehicle** pauses and places the existing orbit camera nearby; **Reset camera** restores the original cell view and orbit distance separately. Add `&vehicle=1` (or `?vehicle=1`) for the collapsed **Vehicle development exercises** panel. Its accelerate/coast/brake/reverse/left/right buttons each run 60 fixed steps. Generated-geometry hiding pauses physics and disables exercises. The panel scrolls when necessary on a small viewport. There is no chase camera, production touch layout or keyboard driving yet; buildings can obscure the simple nearby orbit view.
